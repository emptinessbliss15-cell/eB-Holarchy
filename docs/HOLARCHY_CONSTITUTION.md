# Holarchy Constitution

> Alpha Draft — a living, unratified proposal for discussion, testing, and revision. This document adapts useful practices from Holacracy 5.0 and other governance traditions without automatically adopting any external authority.

## Preamble

We join as autonomous people and interdependent holons to reduce ignorance, expand freedom, meet shared needs, and create prosperity without exploitation. We establish this Holarchy so that people, knowledge, resources, and technology may participate in larger wholes without being reduced to or dominated by those wholes.

Authority under this Constitution exists to serve life, dignity, relationship, and the purposes freely adopted by participants. No office, circle, majority, system, or artificial intelligence is sovereign over a person. Every citizen or partner remains a direct actor in governance.

We intend constructive transition rather than conquest: to build patterns so useful, humane, resilient, and participatory that coercive and extractive patterns become less necessary.

## Article 1 — Constitutional Foundation

### 1.1 Holons

A holon is a whole in its own right and a part of one or more larger wholes. A person, role, circle, agreement, resource, document, proposal, and community may each be modeled as a holon. Containment does not erase autonomy, and autonomy does not erase relationship or responsibility.

### 1.2 Authority of This Constitution

This Constitution defines the default governance rules of the Holarchy. It binds only those who knowingly adopt it or participate in a body that has adopted it, subject to the fundamental rights that cannot be surrendered. No custom, status, wealth, technical control, or unwritten expectation overrides it.

### 1.3 Living Constitution

This Constitution is itself a holon composed of independently addressable provisions. Amendments, interpretations, dissent, supporting evidence, and relationships to external sources must remain visible, attributable, discussable, and revisable.

### 1.4 Interpretation

Interpretation must favor human dignity, direct participation, non-domination, care for the whole, and the least coercive effective means. Where provisions conflict, fundamental rights prevail over organizational convenience, and specific provisions prevail over general ones.

### 1.5 Constructive Transition

The Holarchy need not defeat existing institutions to become useful. Participants may work within, beside, or beyond existing systems while honoring lawful obligations and avoiding needless harm.

## Article 2 — People, Citizenship, and Direct Agency

### 2.1 Direct Governance Actors

Every citizen or partner is a direct governance actor. The Holarchy has no political representatives who replace another person’s voice, consent, vote, judgment, or participation.

### 2.2 Delegation Is Not Representation

A person may delegate a specific task, domain, decision, or role for a defined purpose and duration. Delegation must be explicit, reviewable, and revocable. A delegate exercises assigned authority but does not become the political voice of those affected.

### 2.3 Participation

Participation must be accessible in person or asynchronously wherever practical. A person may speak, propose, object, consent, abstain, record dissent, and request review without surrendering these powers to another.

### 2.4 Membership

Circles may define transparent membership criteria consistent with this Constitution. Entry, suspension, and removal require notice, reasons, a meaningful opportunity to respond, and an avenue of review.

### 2.5 Voluntary Association

No person may be compelled to join a circle or remain in one, except that leaving does not erase freely incurred obligations, responsibility for harm, or lawful stewardship of shared resources.

## Article 3 — Fundamental Rights and Freedoms

### 3.1 Equal Dignity

Every person has equal inherent dignity. The Holarchy may not deny participation, protection, or opportunity on the basis of race, color, ancestry, nationality, sex, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, neurotype, age, religion, conscience, economic condition, or other irrelevant status.

### 3.2 Bodily and Mental Autonomy

Each person controls their body, attention, conscience, identity, and private inner life. Any constraint must be necessary to prevent concrete harm, proportionate, time-limited, and reviewable.

### 3.3 Expression, Inquiry, and Dissent

People may speak, question, investigate, create, assemble, associate, disassociate, and dissent. Restrictions may address concrete harassment, deception, disclosure of protected information, or imminent harm, but may not suppress disagreement merely because it is uncomfortable or unpopular.

### 3.4 Privacy and Data Agency

People have the right to know what personal data is collected, why it is used, who may access it, and how long it is retained. They may inspect, correct, export, and request deletion of personal data, subject to narrowly defined obligations to preserve lawful and accountable records.

### 3.5 Due Process

Before a materially adverse action, a person is entitled to timely notice, the evidence and rule relied upon, a meaningful opportunity to respond, an impartial review, a reasoned decision, and an appeal.

### 3.6 Habeas Corpus

No authority may secretly or arbitrarily detain, confine, exclude, digitally disable, or otherwise deprive a person of liberty. The affected person, or another acting at their request, may demand immediate disclosure of the authority, grounds, location, conditions, and duration of restraint before an independent reviewer empowered to order relief.

### 3.7 Basic Security

The Holarchy should progressively secure access to food, water, shelter, health, safety, communication, learning, and meaningful participation. Need does not diminish citizenship or justify domination.

### 3.8 Remedy

Rights require effective remedies. A person may seek cessation, correction, restoration, restitution, apology, changed practice, or other proportionate repair.

## Article 4 — Roles, Circles, and Domains

### 4.1 Roles

A Role is a holon created to express a Purpose and may hold Accountabilities and Domains. A role grants authority to do its work; it is not a rank, identity, entitlement, or claim of superiority.

### 4.2 Role Authority

A Role Lead may take actions and make decisions reasonably needed to enact the Role’s Purpose or Accountabilities unless constrained by this Constitution, applicable governance, another Role’s Domain, or the fundamental rights of people.

### 4.3 Domains

A Domain is a resource, process, or area of stewardship over which a Role or Circle has exclusive authority to control or regulate. Domains must be explicit. Stewardship does not imply personal ownership and must serve the purpose for which the Domain was entrusted.

### 4.4 Circles

A Circle is a Role expanded to contain Roles, Policies, and participants needed to express its Purpose. Each Circle retains authority not explicitly constrained or delegated and remains accountable to affected people and containing circles.

### 4.5 Subsidiarity

Authority belongs at the smallest, closest, competent holon affected by a decision. A broader Circle may intervene only when effects cross boundaries, rights require protection, shared resources require coordination, or a contained Circle cannot fulfill an adopted obligation.

### 4.6 Circle Links

Information and tensions must flow in both directions between nested Circles. Circle Leads and Circle Links may carry operational information, but they do not replace the direct governance agency of any participant. Any participant retains access to broader governance processes when materially affected.

### 4.7 Policies

A Policy may constrain authority, grant authority, define access to a Domain, or alter a default process where this Constitution permits. Policies must be written, discoverable, attributable, and connected to the Circle and purpose they serve.

## Article 5 — Core Service Roles

### 5.1 Facilitator

The Facilitator protects the integrity and accessibility of governance processes. The Facilitator clarifies the process, invites participation, tests objections against published criteria, limits domination, and helps participants remain with the matter being processed.

The Facilitator is not the decision maker and may not use facilitation authority to determine substantive outcomes, silence valid dissent, or substitute personal judgment for consent.

### 5.2 Secretary

The Secretary stabilizes constitutionally required records and meetings. The Secretary must:

- maintain the Circle’s Roles, Purposes, Accountabilities, Domains, Policies, memberships, proposals, decisions, objections, interpretations, and meeting records;
- publish records promptly in an accessible form, subject to legitimate privacy protections;
- schedule or support constitutionally required meetings and notify eligible participants;
- record provenance, effective dates, superseded provisions, and the authority for each governance change;
- rule on requested interpretations and procedural validity using this Constitution and published governance;
- correct clerical errors without changing substantive meaning and disclose every correction;
- preserve minority statements and unresolved dissent with the decision record;
- provide exports and continuity when the role changes hands.

The Secretary may not create governance by editing a record. Interpretations must be reasoned, attributable, appealable, and treated as provisional when evidence or context is incomplete.

### 5.3 Steward

A Circle may appoint Stewards for resources or Domains. A Steward must disclose material conflicts of interest, maintain usable records, protect the resource from waste or capture, and support orderly transfer.

### 5.4 Role Selection and Removal

Core service roles must be selected by a transparent, consent-based process for a defined term or review interval. A Circle may remove a role holder when continued service creates a demonstrated constraint, provided due process and continuity of records are protected.

## Article 6 — Operational Practice

### 6.1 Tensions

A Tension is a person’s sensed gap between present reality and a possible improvement related to a Purpose. Any participant may process a relevant Tension. No person must prove distress or persuade others that the Tension is important before proposing a next step.

### 6.2 Projects and Next Actions

A Project is an outcome requiring more than one action. A Next Action is a concrete physical or digital act that can be taken. Roles must maintain enough visible operational information for affected people to understand commitments and dependencies.

### 6.3 Requests and Duties of Transparency

Participants may request information, projects, next actions, relative priorities, forecasts, and clarification from Roles when relevant to shared work. Responses must be timely and truthful, while respecting privacy, safety, and reasonable workload.

### 6.4 Individual Initiative

A person may act outside normal role authority when immediate action is reasonably necessary to protect life, rights, critical resources, or the Circle’s capacity to fulfill its Purpose and ordinary authorization is impractical. The actor must minimize harm, avoid irreversible commitments where possible, promptly disclose the action, and accept review.

### 6.5 Relational Agreements

People may make explicit agreements about how they relate while working together. Such agreements must describe observable behavior, remain voluntary, and may not secretly assign Role work, suppress constitutional rights, or bind people who did not consent.

## Article 7 — Proposals, Consent, and Dissent

### 7.1 Proposal Right

Any eligible participant may propose a governance change within the affected Circle. A proposal must identify the Tension, the requested change, its intended scope, and available supporting evidence.

### 7.2 Consent

Consent means no participant has established a valid objection under this Constitution. It does not require preference, enthusiasm, unanimity of opinion, or prediction that a proposal is ideal.

### 7.3 Valid Objection

An objection is valid when the objector presents a reasoned case that adopting the proposal would create or materially worsen a concrete risk to a Purpose, Accountability, Domain, fundamental right, or the Holarchy’s capacity to adapt, and the concern is relevant now rather than a merely imagined possibility without supporting evidence.

A proposal that violates this Constitution always supports an objection. The strength of required evidence should be proportionate to the likelihood, severity, and reversibility of the claimed harm.

### 7.4 Integration

The proposer and objector must seek the smallest change that resolves the valid objection while preserving as much of the proposal as practical. The Facilitator supports integration but may not impose a preferred solution.

### 7.5 Recorded Dissent

When a concern is not a blocking objection, the participant may require it to be recorded with evidence and conditions that would trigger review. Consent does not erase dissent.

### 7.6 Safe-to-Try and Reversibility

Where uncertainty remains, Circles should prefer bounded, observable, reversible experiments with explicit measures, review dates, and rollback conditions.

## Article 8 — Meetings and Deliberation

### 8.1 Accessibility

Governance and operational meetings must have clear purposes, notice, accessible participation methods, and records. Asynchronous participation should be available when practical.

### 8.2 Governance Process

A governance process normally includes check-in, agenda building, presentation of a proposal, clarifying questions, reactions, amendment by the proposer, objection testing, integration, and recording of the result.

### 8.3 Operational Process

An operational process normally includes check-in, review of recurring measures, progress updates, identification of constraints, triage of Tensions into projects or next actions, and closing reflection.

### 8.4 Discussion

Discussion must be integrated into governance before public participation is opened broadly. Each proposal or provision should support threaded, attributable discussion tied to the relevant holon. Discussion informs decisions but does not itself silently change governance.

### 8.5 Urgent Decisions

Urgent decisions may use shortened notice only when delay would create concrete harm. Any emergency decision must state its basis, expire automatically within a short defined period, and receive prompt ordinary review.

## Article 9 — Records, Provenance, and Knowledge

### 9.1 Public-by-Default Governance

Constitutions, Roles, Policies, proposals, decisions, objections, interpretations, authorities, and revision histories are inspectable by affected participants by default. Privacy restrictions must be narrow, explained, and reviewable.

### 9.2 Provenance

Every material change must identify who or what proposed it, its source, time, authority, review state, approving process, and relationship to what it changes. Artificial intelligence contributions must be identified as such.

### 9.3 External Sources

External documents remain distinct from adopted governance. Relationships such as informs, adapted from, supports, contrasts with, or supersedes must express how a source relates to an internal provision. Importing a source does not ratify it.

### 9.4 Knowledge Access

Records must be exportable in open, documented formats. No vendor, database, interface, or identity provider may become the sole practical custodian of the Holarchy’s constitutional memory.

### 9.5 Modeling and Impact Analysis

Participants may model proposed changes before adoption. Simulations must disclose assumptions and uncertainty and may not substitute predicted outcomes for human judgment, lived experience, or consent.

## Article 10 — Justice, Conflict, and Process Breakdown

### 10.1 Nonviolence and De-escalation

The Holarchy favors prevention, dialogue, mediation, restorative practice, and the least coercive effective response. Immediate protective action remains available when necessary to stop concrete harm.

### 10.2 Impartial Review

No person may finally judge a dispute in which they have a material personal interest. Review bodies must disclose conflicts, hear affected participants, examine available evidence, and explain decisions.

### 10.3 Restoration and Accountability

Responses to harm should protect those affected, establish truth as well as reasonably possible, repair damage, restore trustworthy participation where feasible, and prevent recurrence. Accountability is not revenge.

### 10.4 Process Breakdown

A Process Breakdown exists when a Circle persistently violates this Constitution, cannot maintain required records or meetings, suppresses participation, or cannot enact its Purpose. A broader Circle or independent review process may impose the narrowest temporary intervention needed to restore function and rights.

### 10.5 Appeals

Interpretations, disciplinary actions, membership decisions, emergency measures, and findings of Process Breakdown may be appealed to an independent process defined in advance. Appeal does not automatically suspend protective measures, but continued restrictions require ongoing justification.

## Article 11 — Economy, Commons, and Technology

### 11.1 Prosperity Without Exploitation

Economic activity must not depend upon domination, hidden extraction, manufactured dependency, or the treatment of people as disposable inputs. Contributors should share fairly in value they help create.

### 11.2 Cooperative Ownership

Enterprises may be organized as worker or multi-stakeholder cooperatives. Each cooperative must define membership, one-member-one-vote protections where applicable, patronage or surplus allocation, capital stewardship, and separation between enterprise ownership and citizenship in the wider Holarchy.

### 11.3 Commons

Resources held in common must have named stewardship, transparent access rules, ecological limits, conflict processes, and protection against private capture.

### 11.4 Technology

Technology must support human agency, accessibility, interoperability, security, privacy, and graceful exit. Significant automated decisions must be inspectable, contestable, and subject to accountable human governance.

### 11.5 Holon Protocol

The Holon Protocol should keep identity, content, relationships, provenance, permissions, and representations portable across implementations. REST, MCP, peer-to-peer, federated, local, web, mobile, spatial, and future interfaces may coexist without any one interface becoming the model itself.

## Article 12 — Federation and Constitutional Change

### 12.1 Federation

Autonomous Holarchies may federate through explicit agreements defining shared purposes, protocols, responsibilities, resources, dispute processes, entry, and exit. Federation does not erase local autonomy or individual rights.

### 12.2 Constitutional Proposals

Any citizen or partner may propose a constitutional amendment. The proposal must identify affected provisions, reasons, evidence, expected effects, risks, reversibility, and relationships to relevant source material.

### 12.3 Ratification

Ratification must use a published direct-participation process open to every eligible citizen or partner. No representative body may ratify on behalf of passive constituents. The process must allow informed review, discussion, objection, integration, and a durable record of consent and dissent.

### 12.4 Protected Core

No amendment may abolish equal human dignity, direct personal agency, due process, habeas corpus, freedom of conscience, the right to dissent, or meaningful exit while continuing to claim authority under this Constitution.

### 12.5 Versions and Effective Dates

Every ratified version must have a stable identifier, effective date, complete change history, and machine-readable structure. Prior versions remain available. A change has no constitutional force until its required process is complete and its effective status is published.

### 12.6 Alpha Transition

During the Alpha period, provisions are hypotheses to test rather than claims of perfected governance. Experiments must preserve fundamental rights, document results, and feed learning back into constitutional revision.

## Sources and Adaptation Notes

This Alpha Draft draws structural inspiration from Holacracy Constitution 5.0, direct-participation principles, sociocratic consent and linking practices, cooperative governance, international human-rights traditions, constitutional due process, restorative justice, subsidiarity, open-source practice, and the Holarchy’s own recorded principles.

External sources inform this draft but do not govern the Holarchy unless a provision is directly adopted through the constitutional process. Future graph relationships should connect each adapted provision to its specific source passages and record whether it was adopted, modified, contrasted, or rejected.
